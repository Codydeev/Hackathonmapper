STUHI Hackathon · When GPS fails

Where did they go after Bluetooth lost them?

A small model that runs on the phone. It reads how the person was walking, the paths, roads and lakes around them, and where they planned to go, then predicts where a search team should look. Pick a real walk from the Hervanta forest, which the model never saw during training, and move the time slider.

What's inside

Three kinds of input go into one small network. It works in metres relative to the walking direction, and runs on the phone with no internet connection.

How they moved
60 s

Positions every 2 s, turned so the walking direction points forward, plus how accurate they were. Speed, turns and stops all show in this track.

The map around them
48 × 48

Paths, roads and water from OpenStreetMap, 10 m per pixel, read by a 4-layer image network. It sees forks ahead, roads to the side and lakes in the way.

Their plan
Destination

If the phone knows where they were heading (a navigation target, a trailhead), the direction and distance to it. Optional.

Output
6 blobs

A cloud for any time from 2 s to 3 min ahead.

How it compares

Area needed to find the person 9 times out of 10 in the Hervanta forest, by time since the Bluetooth link was lost. Every method is held to that same hit rate, so a smaller area is better. Both simple guesses were tuned on the same training walks.

Limits

  • The walks come from people who recorded their hikes, not from people who were lost. Lost people tend to follow trails, streams and other lines in the terrain.
  • In training, "destination" is where each walk really ended. That stands in for a target the person set in the app; real plans change, so treat the destination numbers as a best case.
  • A phone has to fix its 90% line in advance. Lines set on the training forests caught the person less than 90% of the time in Hervanta, for every method, so a real deployment would widen them or calibrate per region.
